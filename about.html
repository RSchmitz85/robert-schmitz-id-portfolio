Background

An instructional designer who ships the whole system

I design learning systems end to end — curriculum maps, assessment banks, LMS course architecture, and the facilitator materials that let someone else run them — and I test them against the hardest audience there is: real learners, every day.

What I design

My working method is the same in any content domain, and it is Dick & Carey's order: analyze the audience and the standard, write the measurable objective, design the evidence that proves it, then build the instruction that gets there. That method has produced 10,000+ standards-aligned assessment items organized into reusable Canvas item banks, a modular Canvas course package adopted district-wide and delivered by instructors who did not build it, QTI-packaged assessment content that moves between Canvas and Schoology as one deliverable, and Real Engagement with Mathematics (REM) — an Algebra 1 curriculum whose core is a 144-lesson Guided Notes course: every lesson a student reconstruction page set the learner completes, paired with a teacher edition in which every response is already annotated. Around that core sit reusable practice-asset systems, worked teacher keys, a coherent visual design language, and a production engine that resolves a lesson's standards, models, and assets before any page renders.

Every system ships with the facilitation layer an instructor needs to run it without the author in the room — worked keys, look-fors, and PD. Designing for the person who delivers the material is as much of the job as designing for the person who learns from it.

Where the subject-matter depth comes from

Eleven-plus years teaching secondary and adult mathematics — Algebra 1, Geometry, intensive and remedial courses, and adult GED — is my analysis phase run live. It is why I can spot the exact step where a learner's route breaks, write distractors that map to real misconceptions instead of random wrong numbers, and design feedback that names the error rather than restating the rule. Lead-teacher mentoring, district PD facilitation, curriculum mapping, and contributing to district progress-monitoring assessments extended that same design work to adult learners and program scale.

The classroom is also where my systems get measured. State assessment results, district benchmarks, and LMS reporting tell me which materials are failing, and I rebuild them on that evidence — revision of the materials themselves, not just the delivery.

How I work

I start with the learner and the performance gap, not the slide deck — and the frameworks show up in the artifacts, not just the resume. ADDIE and Dick & Carey govern the REM build order: benchmark and misconception analysis first, objectives and their checks next, instruction last, with a fail-closed publication gate and assessment data closing the loop. Gagné's events and Gradual Release shape the lesson spine itself — hook, retrieval of prerequisites, modeled examples, then support that fades tier by tier. Mayer's principles govern the lesson media (one routine per short segment, models that carry the mathematics instead of decorating it); UDL and TPACK drive the multiple practice formats and technology choices; ARCS shapes the hooks; and Quality Matters serves as a review lens for online design.

I build in Storyline, Canvas, and Schoology, and I use HTML/CSS when a lighter web solution fits. I also run an AI-assisted, human-reviewed production workflow — AI drafts and screens at volume, and nothing reaches a learner until it is checked against the objective it claims to measure.

What I am looking for

Roles in instructional design, curriculum development, and eLearning — especially in edtech, higher education, and organizational learning — where clear thinking about learners, assessment, and scalable design matters more than decorative production.
