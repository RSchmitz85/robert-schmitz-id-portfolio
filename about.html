Background

An instructional designer who ships the whole system

I design learning systems end to end — curriculum maps, assessment banks, LMS course architecture, and the facilitator materials that let someone else run them — and I test them against the hardest audience there is: real learners, every day.

What I design

My method is the same in any content domain, and it is Dick & Carey's order: analyze the audience and the standard, write the measurable objective, design the evidence that proves it, then build the instruction. That method has produced 10,000+ standards-aligned items in reusable Canvas banks, a course package adopted district-wide and run by instructors who did not build it, and Real Engagement with Mathematics — a 144-lesson Guided Notes course of student reconstruction pages with fully annotated teacher editions, produced by an engine that resolves a lesson's standards, models, and assets before any page renders.

Every system ships with the facilitation layer someone else needs to run it — worked keys, look-fors, PD. Designing for the deliverer is as much of the job as designing for the learner.

Where the depth comes from

Eleven-plus years teaching secondary and adult mathematics is my analysis phase run live: I can spot the exact step where a learner's route breaks, write distractors that map to real misconceptions, and design feedback that names the error instead of restating the rule.

The classroom is also where my systems get measured. State results, district benchmarks, and LMS reporting tell me which materials are failing — and I rebuild the materials themselves on that evidence.

How I work

The frameworks show up in the artifacts, not just the resume. ADDIE and Dick & Carey govern the build order; Gagné's events and Gradual Release shape the lesson spine — hook, retrieval, modeled examples, support fading tier by tier; Mayer governs the media; UDL drives the multiple practice formats; ARCS shapes the hooks; Quality Matters serves as a review lens.

I build in Storyline, Canvas, and Schoology — HTML/CSS when lighter fits — and run an AI-assisted, human-reviewed production workflow: AI drafts at volume, nothing ships unchecked against its objective.

What I am looking for

Roles in instructional design, curriculum development, and eLearning — especially in edtech, higher education, and organizational learning — where clear thinking about learners, assessment, and scalable design matters more than decorative production.
