Background

From classroom practice to instructional design

I design learning systems and experiences with the same priorities I held as a lead math teacher: clarity, purposeful practice, and respect for how people actually learn.

Why instructional design

For more than eleven years I taught middle and high school mathematics—Algebra 1, Geometry, remedial and intensive courses, and adult GED. That work meant diagnosing where understanding broke down, redesigning explanations, aligning instruction to standards, and building assessments that told me something useful about what learners could do next.

Those are instructional design problems. Mentoring colleagues, facilitating professional development, mapping curriculum, and contributing to district assessment design made the shift explicit: I was already analyzing audiences, sequencing content, writing objectives, and iterating on materials under real constraints.

I formalized that path through graduate study (Master’s with a 4.0), the University of Central Florida Professional Certification in Instructional Design, and an Ed.D. in Curriculum & Instruction currently in progress. The goal is not to leave teaching behind—it is to apply what teaching taught me at the scale of programs, courses, and learning products.

How I work

I start with the learner and the performance gap, not the slide deck. Models I use deliberately include ADDIE and Dick & Carey for process discipline; Gagné’s events and Gradual Release for lesson and module architecture; ARCS for motivation; Mayer’s principles for multimedia; UDL and TPACK for accessibility and technology decisions; and Quality Matters as a quality lens for online design.

I build in Storyline, Canvas, and Schoology, and I use HTML/CSS when a lighter web solution fits. Large-scale evidence of curriculum systems thinking appears in Real Engagement with Mathematics (REM)—an Algebra 1 lesson architecture (Activate through Extend) and reusable practice asset systems—presented in this portfolio as instructional design and curriculum architecture, not as a marketplace shop. I also use AI-assisted tools as drafting and brainstorming partners—always with human review for accuracy, tone, and instructional integrity.

What I am looking for

Roles in instructional design, curriculum development, and eLearning—especially in edtech, higher education, and organizational learning—where clear thinking about learners, assessment, and scalable design matters more than decorative production.
