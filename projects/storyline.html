Interactive Algebra 1 eLearning Module

A Storyline experience that turns a high-friction Algebra 1 concept into guided practice with worked examples, checks for understanding, and actionable feedback.

Role: Instructional designer & developer Format: Interactive eLearning Domain: Secondary mathematics
  • Storyline
  • Curriculum system
  • Assessment
  • PD / adult learning
  • AI workflow

Overview

Learners often stall when Algebra 1 shifts from procedures they can mimic to representations they must interpret—especially linear relationships presented across tables, graphs, and equations. This module was designed to reduce cognitive overload, make the “why” of each representation visible, and give learners structured chances to try, err, and revise.

The build is grounded in Robert’s Algebra 1 Storyline work (local project assets and storyboards) and is structured for Articulate Storyline delivery into Canvas or Schoology as SCORM or a web object, with consistent navigation and accessible interaction patterns.

Challenge

Classroom observation and remediation work repeatedly showed the same pattern: students could sometimes compute a slope or plot points, yet struggled to explain what a rate of change meant, choose an appropriate representation, or recover after a mistake. Standalone worksheets and click-through slideshows did not provide the guided practice or timely feedback those learners needed.

The design problem was to create a self-paced module that still felt instructional—not a quiz in disguise—and that respected limited development time and LMS constraints.

Audience

  • Secondary Algebra 1 learners, including students in remedial or intensive supports
  • Learners who may have gaps in prior knowledge and benefit from explicit modeling
  • Teachers or facilitators assigning the module as pre-work, intervention, or review inside an LMS

My role

  • Needs framing from classroom and remediation experience
  • Learning objectives, outline, and interaction design
  • Storyboard and Storyline development
  • Feedback copy and formative check design
  • Accessibility and navigation conventions

Constraints

  • Must run inside common K–12 / higher-ed LMS environments
  • Limited media production budget (no custom video shoot required)
  • Readable on laptop; usable on tablet where possible
  • Content accuracy and standards alignment non-negotiable

Design process

  1. Analyze

    Clarified the performance gap (representation fluency vs. isolated calculation), entry skills, and typical misconceptions drawn from teaching Algebra 1 and intensive courses.

  2. Design

    Wrote measurable objectives, sequenced Gagné-style events (gain attention → present content → guided practice → independent practice → feedback), and sketched branched feedback for common errors.

  3. Develop

    Built screens in Storyline with consistent chrome, keyboard-friendly controls, and worked-example → try-it cycles. Drafted feedback that names the error type, not only “try again.”

  4. Evaluate (formative)

    Reviewed against multimedia principles (signaling, segmenting, coherence) and Quality Matters–aligned online design checks; revised wording and pacing based on walkthroughs.

Design decisions

  • Worked examples before problem sets Follows evidence on worked-example effect for novices; fades support as confidence grows (gradual release).
  • One idea per screen, clear signaling Applies Mayer’s segmenting and signaling principles to keep visual attention on the representation that matters.
  • Feedback that teaches Incorrect responses trigger brief corrective explanation tied to the misconception, then a chance to retry—supporting formative assessment, not mere scoring.
  • Motivation via relevance and confidence ARCS-informed framing: why the skill matters in later algebra, plus early wins before higher-complexity items.

Solution

The module opens with a short orientation and objective screen, then moves through short instructional segments that juxtapose table, graph, and equation views of the same relationship. Learners complete guided interactions (match representations, predict a next value, interpret a slope in context) before a short independent practice set with teaching feedback.

Navigation is linear by default with optional review of prior screens, so learners who need to revisit a model can do so without hunting through a maze of menus.

Learning principles applied

Gagné’s events of instruction

Structured attention, presentation, guidance, practice, and feedback as deliberate module beats.

Mayer’s multimedia principles

Segmenting, signaling, and coherence to reduce extraneous load on math screens.

Gradual Release (GRR)

I do → we do → you do pacing inside interactive sequences.

ARCS motivation

Relevance and confidence built before higher-stakes independent items.

Outcome

The module provides a reusable pattern for concept-focused Algebra 1 eLearning: clear objectives, representation-centered instruction, and feedback that supports learning. It is suitable as intervention, review, or flipped pre-work depending on the facilitator’s pacing plan.

No fabricated completion rates or score gains are claimed here. Impact should be measured in context once deployed with a real learner cohort.

Reflection

Teaching intensive and remedial Algebra 1 made the cost of vague feedback obvious. In Storyline, that insight became a design rule: every incorrect path should leave the learner smarter about the mathematics, not only aware that they were wrong. Next iterations would add optional challenge paths for learners who demonstrate early mastery, without forcing everyone through the same density of scaffolding.
