Curriculum design

Algebra 1 curriculum architecture

A shared lesson structure connects worked examples, guided practice, independent checks, and teacher support.

Available to inspect: representative REM student and teacher materials

Context
Independent work for Real Engagement with Mathematics (REM).
Audience
Secondary Algebra 1 learners and the teachers facilitating their practice.
My contribution
Course structure, lesson and assessment design, student materials, and annotated teacher support.

Inspect the lesson design

Unit 1, Lesson 1: solving one-step equations. Read the student task beside its matching teacher support. Each page opens at full resolution.

Three design decisions

  • Fade the support within the practiceA first move lowers the entry barrier. Later items remove the cue so the learner has to select the operation. The readiness check asks for substitution into the original equation.
  • Give the facilitator the reasoningThe annotated edition shows both the answer and how to reach it. This helps a teacher distinguish a calculation error from an incorrect inverse operation.
  • Keep a consistent lesson rhythmActivate → Model → Guide → Apply → Reflect → Extend gives different topics a recognizable structure. The content and representations still need lesson-specific review.

Course structure and evidence

What this work demonstrates

The selected pages show how I connect practice, feedback, and facilitator support. They provide direct evidence of design choices and material production.

What remains to evaluate

A learner study would examine where prompts are still needed, how accurately students explain their checks, and whether teachers can use the materials without additional clarification. Learner gains and district adoption are not measured here.

Production approach

Shared lesson metadata and templates help keep materials consistent. Student and teacher versions still require content and visual review before release.

Rally Coach: two roles, shared reasoning

A promotional role demonstration for partner practice. The dashboard-style graphics illustrate the routine; they are not collected participation data.

Read the transcript and visual description
  1. 0:00–0:10 — The opening introduces partner practice with a role for each learner. A stylized participation graphic contrasts unstructured pairs with assigned roles; it is an illustration, not measured results.
  2. 0:10–0:21 — Two roles appear: the Driver writes and explains the work; the Coach predicts a move, asks for reasoning, and verifies the proof. The prompt is one pencil, two minds.
  3. 0:21–0:33 — The coaching guide models three prompts: name the next move before it is written; ask what keeps the equation balanced; substitute to check the result.
  4. 0:33–0:41 — A pit-stop graphic cues a role switch: new Driver, new Coach. Both students take turns doing and explaining the mathematics.
  5. 0:41–0:52 — A teacher page and a stylized monitoring display illustrate what the facilitator can look for. The activity cover closes the clip with the prompt to print, pair, and coach the math.

Micro-lesson: solve and check a linear equation

A narrated worked example with synchronized captions. The source clip ends during its closing promotional sentence.

Read the transcript and visual description
  1. 0:00–0:02 — The screen asks the learner to solve 4x + 5 = −19. Narration: “Can your students solve this?”
  2. 0:02–0:08 — Subtract 5 from both sides. The equation becomes 4x = −24.
  3. 0:08–0:14 — Divide both sides by 4. The solution is x = −6.
  4. 0:14–0:21 — Check by substitution: 4(−6) + 5 = −19. Since −24 + 5 = −19, both sides agree. Narration: “Check it. Four times negative six is negative twenty-four, plus five is negative nineteen. It works.”
  5. 0:21–0:25 — The REM logo appears. Closing narration begins: “Full Algebra 1 lessons and practice on…” and cuts off at the end of the source clip.
