Adult learning

Practice-centered professional development

A 60-minute workshop plan helps educators adapt a self-checking practice routine to an upcoming lesson and plan its first use.

Representative design sample · Session plan and transfer job aid

Context
A portfolio session design informed by teaching, mentoring, and facilitation experience. It is not presented as a documented delivered event.
Audience
Secondary educators with different levels of familiarity with the practice formats.
My contribution
Performance outcome, facilitation sequence, collaborative task, and transfer support.
Available evidence
The agenda and job aid below. Attendance, participant feedback, and classroom-transfer results have not been documented for this sample.

Inspect the session plan

Intended outcome: given an upcoming lesson, the participant adapts one practice structure, explains how students will verify their work, and identifies the first classroom use.

Session agenda — 60 minutes outcome: leave with one practice structure planned for your own class
  • 0–5
    Frame the gap

    One real student-work sample; what feedback did this learner get, and when?

  • 5–15
    Model

    Facilitator walks one self-checking structure with actual lesson materials — with the learner routine visible.

  • 15–40
    Collaborative practice

    Pairs adapt the structure to a lesson from their own upcoming week; facilitator coaches.

  • 40–50
    Debrief the design

    What did you change and why? Surface adaptation decisions, not compliance.

  • 50–60
    Commit & transfer

    Each participant names the class period and date they will run it; leaves with the look-fors aid.

Transfer job aid look-fors for the first classroom run
  • Students can say how they know an answer is rightLearners can explain the verification step
  • Feedback arrives during practice, while the learner can still respondThe structure — decoder, key, arrival signal — does the checking
  • Work is shown, alongside the recorded answersThe self-check rewards the route, not the guess
  • Wrong answers produce a next actionLearners know what to re-try, and identify the step to revisit
  • One thing to adjust before the second runIteration is the expectation, and guides the next revision

Three choices that support transfer

  • Use participants’ upcoming lessonsAdults bring useful domain knowledge. Adapting a real task makes the practice immediately relevant and produces something they can use.
  • Reserve 25 minutes for collaborative adaptationThe model is brief. Most working time goes to making a design decision, trying it with a colleague, and receiving facilitator feedback.
  • Leave with look-fors and a first-use planThe job aid focuses observation on feedback timing, visible reasoning, and the next adjustment. A named class and date turn the workshop task into a concrete follow-up.

Planned evaluation

Before a first delivery, check that the adaptation task fits the 25-minute block and that participants can use the job aid independently. During a pilot, review each adapted activity against the outcome. After the first classroom use, collect a short reflection about what worked, where learners needed support, and what the teacher changed.

These are proposed evaluation steps. They do not represent completed workshop or transfer results.
