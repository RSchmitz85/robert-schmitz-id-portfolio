Workplace learning · Independent portfolio concept

Make the next conversation more useful.

Practice responding to a missed handoff with a specific observation, a clear impact, and a question that opens a path forward.

My role: I designed the scenario, response-specific feedback, and transfer aid. This is an independent portfolio project for new people managers.

Audience: New people managersFormat: Three-decision scenario + job aidStatus: Concept · not learner-tested
Try the scenario ↓Read the conversation aid

The moment

A handoff was missed. What do you say?

You manage a project team. Yesterday, Alex’s handoff notes arrived after the agreed 3 p.m. deadline, delaying another team’s review. You do not yet know what caused the delay. You are meeting privately with Alex.

Explore three decisions: open the conversation, understand the obstacle, and agree on a handoff plan.

Take it into the conversation

Observe. Ask. Agree.

  1. Describe what happened.

    “We agreed on ___. I observed ___. The impact was ___.”

  2. Ask and listen.

    “What got in the way? What am I missing?”

  3. Agree on the next action.

    “What is a feasible next step? What support is needed, and when will we check in?”

Inside the design

A useful next step beats a verdict.

The target skill is reaching a workable handoff agreement without assuming why the first handoff failed. Opening choices change Alex’s response. The next decision contrasts checking the dependency, reinforcing the deadline, and taking over. The final decision tests whether the agreement includes ownership, timing, and an early warning.

Generic response

“Incorrect. Try again.”

The learner knows the choice missed the target, but receives no guidance about why.

Response-specific guidance

“Replace the judgment with the agreed time, the observed delay, and a question about the cause.”

The feedback identifies the issue and supplies an action the learner can rehearse.

Illustrative design comparison, not a claim about a previously tested version.

Why a scenario and job aid?

The scenario makes the distinction visible in a realistic choice. The job aid supports generating a response in a new situation, where no answer choices are available.

Evidence and next evaluation

Checked behavior. A defined next test.

This independent concept demonstrates a three-decision conversation, response-specific feedback, and a transfer aid. Workplace outcomes have not been measured.
