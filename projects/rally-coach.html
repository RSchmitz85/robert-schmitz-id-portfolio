The learner experience

What happens on the page?

Partners alternate between solving and coaching. The solver names a function family and points to a graph clue; the coach uses an Expect, Ask, Verify routine to question the choice and require evidence. They finish by comparing graph shapes and creating a graph for their partner to identify.

  1. 01

    Identify the family

    Name the function family and explain the visible graph clue that supports your choice.

  2. 02

    Coach the next move

    Ask a focused question and check that the family name matches the whole graph shape.

  3. 03

    Switch and connect

    Trade roles, compare cubic and cube root graphs, and sketch a family for your partner to identify.

Why I designed it this way

Character gets attention.
The task does the teaching.

Teach coaching as a skill

The coaching school models what to listen for, what to ask, and how to verify. The partner has a defined job beyond announcing whether the answer is right.

Keep both learners accountable

Role changes make each learner rehearse the full process. Partner pages distinguish the learner’s own problem from the coaching card for the other partner’s task.

Beyond this lesson

The structure carries into peer training: attempt a task, explain the decision, receive a targeted coaching question, then verify before switching roles.

Evidence & next evaluation

These authored materials show the activity structure and design decisions. Learner outcomes have not yet been measured for this case study.

What I would check next

Observe whether coaches ask useful questions before revealing answers, and use an individual exit task to distinguish shared success from independent understanding.
