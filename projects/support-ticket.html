Resolve Your First Support Ticket

Audience and problem

Closing a ticket is a performance decision.

New internal help-desk employees at a fictional logistics company need a repeatable method for resolving printing issues. A completed troubleshooting action does not establish that work has resumed.

The designed performance combines impact-based prioritization, relevant guidance, an approved action, useful notes and verification before closure.

Learning objectives

By the end, the learner should be able to:

  • Use a company matrix to assign priority and choose guidance that fits the evidence.
  • Perform an approved action and distinguish an action log from a verified result.
  • Document the actual work and choose a status supported by the current employee confirmation.

Role and tools: self-directed instructional design and development; PowerPoint layouts, native Storyline controls and variables, HyperFrames video production, and AI-assisted writing, illustration and build automation. No client or learner-impact results are implied.

My instructional design process

ADDIE, from ticket to verified resolution.

I organized the design around observable work: assess impact, use the right guidance, take an approved action and verify the result. Each phase supports that same workflow.

Analysis

Define the performance gap.

An action log does not prove resolution.

For this self-directed concept, I defined a plausible gap: a new help-desk employee may complete a troubleshooting action and close the ticket before checking whether the employee can work again. Priority, evidence and documentation also affect the quality of the resolution.

Northline Logistics, Relay Desk and the company rules are fictional design assumptions. I broke the work into five observable criteria and supplied a priority matrix and knowledge base so the task tests judgment within a defined process.

02

Design

Move from help to independence.

Change the issue; retain the workflow.

I sequenced demonstration, guided practice and an independent challenge. The guided case fixes one workstation’s printer selection. The challenge introduces a stalled label queue, six affected packers and a dispatch deadline, requiring a different article and action.

Five criteria earn 20 points each. Passing requires 80/100 plus an approved action and current verification before closure. Structured documentation makes the assessment observable; editable prose and reflection remain unscored.

03

Development

Build a usable practice space.

Make every field reflect the current attempt.

I developed editable PowerPoint layouts and a 41-slide native Storyline simulation with ticket records, knowledge-base articles, action controls, notes and status fields. Variables retain the submitted work; a new action clears old verification and closure so an earlier confirmation cannot justify the latest change.

Poly and Poppins support the visual hierarchy, while three captioned demonstrations orient the learner. Production review led to refinements to the submission and debrief controls.

Assessment approach

Evaluate the work that was actually done.

Five criteria earn 20 points each: priority, article, action, documentation and verified closure. Passing requires 80/100 plus an approved action and verification before the ticket is resolved.

Structured documentation is evaluated against actual activity. The editable template and optional reflection do not imply natural-language grading. A new action invalidates previous verification and closure.

Practice structure

  1. DemonstrateIntroduce Relay Desk and a worked example.
  2. GuideFix one workstation’s printer selection.
  3. TransferResolve a different stalled-queue ticket independently.
  4. DebriefReview the submitted work against each criterion.

Guided practice provides immediate help. The independent task retains the resources and removes step-by-step hints.
