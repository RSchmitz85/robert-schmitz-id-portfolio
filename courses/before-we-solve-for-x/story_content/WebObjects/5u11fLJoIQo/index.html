What is the confidence rating about?

Self-efficacy is a learner’s belief that they can do a particular task. A student gives a 2 before solving a written equation. Later, they give a 4 before a multiple-choice ribbon problem with the same quantities.

What can you conclude from those ratings?

Check for independent transfer

After students explain their strategies to a partner, give each a comparable task. This time use x + ⅝ = 1.

Use both signals to choose the next lesson

On the independent check, Student A still rates confidence 4 and writes ½ for x + ⅝ = 1. Student B stays at 1 and uses ⅝ + ⅜ = 1 to check the correct answer.

Which next move fits the evidence?

Take the ladder lesson to class

  1. Before solving. Ask “How sure are you that you can solve this equation on your own?” Use the same 1–4 scale each time, with 1 meaning “not sure” and 4 meaning “very sure.”
  2. Read the work. Look for the inverse operation, the fraction reasoning, and a check. Compare each one with the student’s prediction.
  3. Teach the prerequisite. Fold a strip into four equal spaces. Have students explain how the distance from ¾ to 1 represents x. Name a successful step and let partners explain how they checked it.
  4. Check again tomorrow. Ask whether the model felt useful. Separately, collect a fresh rating and independent written work on x + ⅝ = 1 in the same symbolic format. Look for ⅜ supported by reasoning.
  5. Choose what comes next. A higher rating with the same error calls for another probe of fraction meaning. Sound work with a low rating calls for specific feedback and another chance to succeed independently.
