Let’s say I put this on the board. X plus three fourths equals one. Before anybody picks up a pencil, I ask, “How sure are you that you can solve this equation on your own?” One student tells me they’re pretty sure. They subtract three fourths from both sides, but get the fraction part wrong. Another student says they’re barely sure at all and gets one fourth, with work that shows exactly why. If I only look at who got it right, I miss something I could use to help both students.

Which plan lets you compare confidence and reasoning across the two attempts?

Take the ladder lesson to class

  1. Before solving. Ask “How sure are you that you can solve this equation on your own?” Use the same 1–4 scale each time, with 1 meaning “not sure” and 4 meaning “very sure.”
  2. Read the work. Look for the inverse operation, the fraction reasoning, and a check. Compare each one with the student’s prediction.
  3. Teach the missing step. Have students count equal intervals and explain how the distance from ¾ to 1 represents x.
  4. Check again. Collect a fresh rating and independent written work on x + ⅝ = 1. Look for ⅜ supported by reasoning. Use both pieces of evidence to decide what to teach next.
