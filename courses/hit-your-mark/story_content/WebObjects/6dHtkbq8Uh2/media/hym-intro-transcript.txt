HIT YOUR MARK - INTRODUCTION (about 1 minute)

Three. Two. One. Hit your mark.

First, the stage. Say he walks at the same speed the whole way.

Every second, he gets three quarters of a meter closer to the mark.

Next, the pool. The swimmer pushes off the wall and keeps the same speed.

Every second, the swimmer gets one and a half meters farther from the start.

One distance goes down. The other goes up.

But they're both straight lines.

The slope is the rate. That's how much the distance changes every second.

The y-intercept is where the line hits the y-axis, right at zero seconds.

He starts six meters from the mark. The swimmer starts three meters from the wall.

That's where you are when the clock starts.

Change either one, and the whole line moves.

Now, here's the problem.

The director wants him there sooner.

The coach wants a faster swim.

Stick with the old plan, and you'll miss.

So what do you change? The rate, or the start?

You'll do both. The stage first, then the pool.

Graph it, adjust it, and hit your mark.
