HIT YOUR MARK - INTRODUCTION (about 1 minute)

Three. Two. One. Hit your mark.

On stage, a performer walks toward a tape mark on the floor.

Every second, the distance to the mark shrinks.

In the pool, a swimmer pushes off the wall and glides.

Every second, the distance swum grows.

One number goes down. One goes up.

But both of them are lines.

The slope is the rate: how much it changes each second.

The intercept is the start: where you are when the clock begins.

Change either one, and the whole line moves.

Now here's the problem.

The director moves the cue earlier.

The coach wants a faster split.

Keep the old plan, and you miss.

Early is wrong. Late is wrong.

So what do you change: the rate, or the start?

Choose your scene. Build the model. Graph it, adjust it, and hit your mark.
