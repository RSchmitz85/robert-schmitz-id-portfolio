HIT YOUR MARK - INTRODUCTION (about 1 minute)

Three. Two. One. Hit your mark.

On stage, a performer walks toward a tape mark on the floor.

Every second, the distance to the mark drops three quarters of a meter.

In the pool, a swimmer pushes off the wall and glides.

Every second, the distance from the start grows one and a half meters.

One number goes down. One goes up.

But both of them are lines.

The slope is the rate: how much the distance changes each second.

The y-intercept is where the line crosses the y-axis, at zero seconds.

Six meters from the mark on stage. Three meters from the start in the pool.

That's where you are when the clock starts.

Change either one, and the whole line moves.

Now, here's the problem.

The director moves the cue earlier.

The coach wants a faster split.

Keep the old plan, and you miss.

So what do you change: the rate, or the start?

Choose your scene. Build the model. Graph it, adjust it, and hit your mark.

The performer stands at the start position with the clock at 0 s and 6 m to go; the swimmer is at 3 m with the clock at 0 s. As the clock starts, both clips begin moving and each dot leaves the y-axis. One line pivots (RATE) and another slides up and down (START). NOW HERE'S THE PROBLEM.
