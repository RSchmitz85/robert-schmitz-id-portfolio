Algebra 1 / 7.4 · Compare sequences and models

7.4 · Compare sequences and models

Learning target

Distinguish arithmetic and geometric sequences and write rules.

Think about the idea

Arithmetic sequences add a constant difference; geometric sequences multiply by a constant ratio. If n begins at 1, explicit rules are aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d and aₙ = a₁r^(n−1). A recursive rule also needs a starting value. A model should match the observed pattern and a plausible mechanism.

Worked example

Compare 4, 7, 10, 13 with 4, 8, 16, 32.

  1. The first has constant difference 3: aₙ = 4 + 3(n−1).
  2. The second has constant ratio 2: bₙ = 4·2^(n−1).
  3. Their fourth terms are 13 and 32. Equal starting values do not imply equal growth.

Spot and repair an error

A learner writes aₙ = 4·2ⁿ for the second sequence with n = 1 as the first term.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

That gives 8 at n=1. Use n−1 in the exponent when the first term is 4.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Write the next two terms of 5, 9, 13.

Check the reasoning

17, 21: add 4 each time.

Practice 2

Write an explicit rule for 3, 9, 27 starting at n=1.

Check the reasoning

aₙ = 3·3^(n−1), equivalently 3ⁿ.

Practice 3

Compare adding 10% then subtracting 10% to the starting value.

Check the reasoning

The combined multiplier is 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99, leaving 99% of the original.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
