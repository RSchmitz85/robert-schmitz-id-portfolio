Algebra 1 / 6.4 · Model systems and regions

6.4 · Model systems and regions

Learning target

Use simultaneous equations or inequalities to model constraints.

Think about the idea

Define both variables with units. Equations model exact conditions, while inequalities model limits. A system of inequalities uses the overlap of half-planes. A solid boundary includes equality; a dashed boundary excludes it. Test a point to identify the correct side of each boundary.

Worked example

A group buys 10 tickets: adults cost $8 and students $5. Total revenue is $62.

  1. Let a and s count adult and student tickets. Write a + s = 10 and 8a + 5s = 62.
  2. Substitute s = 10 − a: 8a + 50 − 5a = 62, so a = 4.
  3. Then s = 6. Check: 4 + 6 = 10 and 32 + 30 = 62.

Spot and repair an error

A learner uses negative ticket counts because they satisfy one transformed equation.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Both original equations and nonnegative integer constraints must be satisfied.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Does (2, 3) satisfy x + y ≤ 6 and y ≥ x?

Check the reasoning

Yes: 5 ≤ 6 and 3 ≥ 2.

Practice 2

Describe the region x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, x + y ≤ 4.

Check the reasoning

The first-quadrant triangle including its boundaries, with vertices (0,0), (4,0), (0,4).

Practice 3

Two items cost $3 and $5. Eight items total $30. How many of each?

Check the reasoning

5 of the $3 item and 3 of the $5 item: x+y=8 and 3x+5y=30.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
