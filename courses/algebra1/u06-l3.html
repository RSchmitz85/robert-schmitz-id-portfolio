6.3 · Solve by elimination

Learning target

Add equivalent equations to eliminate one variable.

Think about the idea

Adding equations preserves an equality that every shared solution must satisfy. Opposite coefficients cancel. If needed, multiply an entire equation by a nonzero number to create opposites. After finding one variable, return to an original equation to find the other.

Worked example

Solve 2x + y = 11 and 3x − y = 9.

  1. Add the equations: 5x = 20.
  2. x = 4. Substitute in 2x + y = 11.
  3. 8 + y = 11 gives y = 3; check 12 − 3 = 9.

Spot and repair an error

A learner multiplies only the x term when scaling an equation.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Multiply every term on both sides. Otherwise the new equation is not equivalent.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Solve x + y = 7 and x − y = 1.

Check the reasoning

(4, 3): add to get 2x = 8.

Practice 2

Solve 2x + y = 8 and x − y = 1.

Check the reasoning

(3, 2): adding gives 3x = 9.

Practice 3

Solve x + 2y = 8 and 3x − 2y = 8.

Check the reasoning

(4, 2): add to get 4x = 16, then substitute.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
