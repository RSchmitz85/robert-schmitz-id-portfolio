Algebra 1 / 10.3 · Fit and critique linear models

10.3 · Fit and critique linear models

Learning target

Interpret a fitted line and compute a residual.

Think about the idea

A scatter plot displays paired quantitative data. A fitted line describes a trend, not an exact rule for every point. A residual is observed output minus predicted output. A positive residual is above the model. Predictions far outside the observed input range are extrapolations and need extra justification.

Worked example

A study-time model predicts score y = 5x + 50. A student studying 4 hours scores 76.

  1. Predicted score at x=4 is 5(4)+50=70.
  2. Residual = 76−70=6 points.
  3. The observation is 6 points above the fitted line. A prediction of 110 at 12 hours would exceed a 100-point scale.

Spot and repair an error

A learner claims the line proves every extra study hour causes exactly 5 more points.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

A fitted association is not a guaranteed individual response or causal effect. Confounding variables and model error remain possible.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

For ŷ=2x+3 at x=5, observed y=11. Find the residual.

Check the reasoning

Prediction 13; residual 11−13=−2.

Practice 2

What does a negative residual mean?

Check the reasoning

The observed point is below the model prediction.

Practice 3

Data cover ages 12–16. Is a prediction at age 40 interpolation?

Check the reasoning

No, it is extrapolation far outside the observed range.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
