1.3 · Build equivalent expressions

Learning target

Use distribution and like terms to rewrite an expression.

Think about the idea

Equivalent expressions have the same value for every permitted input. Distribution multiplies every term inside the parentheses. Like terms have the same variable part, including exponents. Combine their coefficients; do not combine unlike powers.

Worked example

Rewrite 3(2x − 4) + 5x.

  1. Distribute 3 to both terms: 6x − 12 + 5x.
  2. Combine the x terms: (6 + 5)x − 12.
  3. The equivalent expression is 11x − 12. At x = 2, both originals and rewrites equal 10.

Spot and repair an error

A learner rewrites −2(x − 3) as −2x − 6.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The second product is (−2)(−3) = +6, so the result is −2x + 6.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Simplify 4y + 3 − y + 8.

Check the reasoning

3y + 11: combine 4y − y and 3 + 8 separately.

Practice 2

Expand 5(2a + 1).

Check the reasoning

10a + 5: multiply both terms by 5.

Practice 3

Simplify 2(x + 4) − 3x.

Check the reasoning

8 − x: 2x + 8 − 3x = −x + 8.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
