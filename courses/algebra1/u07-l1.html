Algebra 1 / 7.1 · Use exponent rules

7.1 · Use exponent rules

Learning target

Rewrite powers using product, quotient, and power rules.

Think about the idea

For the same nonzero base, multiplying powers adds exponents and dividing powers subtracts them. Raising a power to a power multiplies exponents. A nonzero base to power zero is 1; a negative exponent indicates a reciprocal. These rules do not turn a sum of powers into one power.

Worked example

Simplify (x³ · x⁴)/x² for x ≠ 0.

  1. Multiply the numerator powers: x³ · x⁴ = x⁷.
  2. Divide by x²: x⁷/x² = x⁵.
  3. For x = 2, both expressions equal 32. The original denominator excludes zero.

Spot and repair an error

A learner writes x³ + x⁴ = x⁷.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Adding exponents applies to multiplication with a common base, not addition.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Simplify (a²)³.

Check the reasoning

a⁶: multiply exponents 2 × 3.

Practice 2

Evaluate 2⁻³.

Check the reasoning

1/8: the negative exponent means 1/(2³).

Practice 3

Simplify b⁵/b⁵ for b ≠ 0.

Check the reasoning

1, because b⁰ = 1 for nonzero b.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
