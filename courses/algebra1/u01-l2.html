Algebra 1 / 1.2 · Evaluate expressions

1.2 · Evaluate expressions

Learning target

Evaluate an expression accurately for a given input.

Think about the idea

A variable represents a quantity that can change. Substitute the given value wherever that variable occurs. Parentheses preserve a negative value when it is squared. Evaluate powers before multiplication, then addition and subtraction.

Worked example

Evaluate 2x² − 3x + 1 when x = −2.

  1. Substitute with parentheses: 2(−2)² − 3(−2) + 1.
  2. The square is 4, so the expression becomes 8 + 6 + 1.
  3. The value is 15. The expression is not an equation to solve.

Spot and repair an error

A learner writes (−2)² = −4.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Squaring means (−2)(−2), which equals positive 4. By contrast, −2² means −(2²).

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Evaluate 3a − 5 when a = 4.

Check the reasoning

7: 3(4) − 5 = 12 − 5.

Practice 2

Evaluate b² + 2b when b = −3.

Check the reasoning

3: (−3)² + 2(−3) = 9 − 6.

Practice 3

Evaluate (m + 2)/3 when m = 7.

Check the reasoning

3: (7 + 2)/3 = 9/3.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
