Algebra 1 / 3.3 · Combine constraints

3.3 · Combine constraints

Learning target

Interpret compound inequalities using and and or.

Think about the idea

An and statement requires both conditions, so its solution is their overlap. An or statement requires at least one condition, so take their union. In a chained inequality, apply the same operation to all three parts. Include or exclude each endpoint according to its symbol.

Worked example

Solve 2 < x + 3 ≤ 8.

  1. Subtract 3 from all three parts.
  2. −1 < x ≤ 5.
  3. This is an open boundary at −1 and a closed boundary at 5, with the interval between them included.

Spot and repair an error

A learner interprets x < 1 or x > 4 as the values between 1 and 4.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Between 1 and 4 satisfies neither part. The solution is the two outside regions, excluding both endpoints.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Solve 3 ≤ x − 2 < 7.

Check the reasoning

5 ≤ x < 9: add 2 throughout.

Practice 2

Does x = 2 satisfy x < 0 or x ≥ 2?

Check the reasoning

Yes, because it satisfies the second condition.

Practice 3

Combine x > 1 and x ≤ 5.

Check the reasoning

1 < x ≤ 5, the intersection of the two sets.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
