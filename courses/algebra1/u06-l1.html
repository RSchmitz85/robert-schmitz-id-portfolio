6.1 · Interpret intersections

Learning target

Identify a solution that satisfies both equations.

Think about the idea

A system asks for values that make all its equations true simultaneously. On a graph a shared solution is an intersection. Parallel distinct lines have no shared point; identical lines have infinitely many. An estimated intersection should be checked algebraically.

Worked example

Solve y = x + 1 and y = −x + 5 by locating a shared point.

  1. At x = 2, the first rule gives y = 3.
  2. The second rule also gives y = −2 + 5 = 3.
  3. The lines intersect at (2, 3); both coordinates are required.

Spot and repair an error

A learner reports x = 2 as the complete solution.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

A two-variable system needs an ordered pair. Substitute to find y and verify both equations.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Does (1, 4) solve y = 2x + 2 and y = −x + 5?

Check the reasoning

Yes. Both equations give y = 4 at x = 1.

Practice 2

Classify y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x − 4.

Check the reasoning

No solution: same slope, different intercepts.

Practice 3

Classify x + y = 5 and 2x + 2y = 10.

Check the reasoning

Infinitely many shared solutions: the second equation is twice the first.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
