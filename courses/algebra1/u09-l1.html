9.1 · Read a parabola

Learning target

Interpret vertex form, symmetry, and intercepts.

Think about the idea

A quadratic function has a squared variable term and a parabolic graph. In y = a(x−h)²+k, the vertex is (h,k) and the axis of symmetry is x=h. Positive a opens upward and negative a downward. Factored form reveals zeros; evaluating at x=0 gives the y-intercept.

Worked example

Analyze y = (x − 2)² − 4.

  1. The vertex is (2,−4), the axis is x=2, and the parabola opens upward.
  2. For y=0: (x−2)²=4 gives x=0 or x=4.
  3. A symmetric table for x=0,1,2,3,4 gives y=0,−3,−4,−3,0.

Spot and repair an error

A learner reports vertex (−2,−4).

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The square is zero at x=2, not −2. The expression is x−h, so h=2.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Find the vertex of y = (x + 3)² + 1.

Check the reasoning

(−3,1), since x+3 is zero at x=−3.

Practice 2

Does y = −2(x−1)² + 5 have a maximum or minimum?

Check the reasoning

A maximum of 5 at x=1, because the leading multiplier is negative.

Practice 3

Find the y-intercept of y = x² − 6x + 8.

Check the reasoning

(0,8), by setting x=0.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
