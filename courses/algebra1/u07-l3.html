7.3 · Build exponential models

Learning target

Model constant percent change with an initial value and multiplier.

Think about the idea

An exponential model y = a·bᵗ has initial value a at t = 0 and a constant multiplier b for equal input steps. Growth by rate r uses b = 1 + r; decay uses b = 1 − r. A percent must be converted to a decimal. Discrete compounding applies at the stated intervals.

Worked example

A collection begins with 200 samples and grows by 10% per day. Model and evaluate after 2 days.

  1. The daily multiplier is 1 + 0.10 = 1.10.
  2. N(t) = 200(1.10)ᵗ for whole days t ≥ 0.
  3. N(2) = 200(1.21) = 242. The second increase is 10% of 220, not of 200.

Spot and repair an error

A learner uses 200 + 20t as the exponential growth rule.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

That adds a fixed 20 each day. Percent growth multiplies the current amount, so daily additions change.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Model a $500 value decreasing 20% per year.

Check the reasoning

V(t) = 500(0.80)ᵗ; the retained fraction is 80%.

Practice 2

A population doubles each hour from 3. Find the value at hour 4.

Check the reasoning

3(2⁴) = 48.

Practice 3

A model is 80(1.05)ᵗ. Interpret its constants.

Check the reasoning

Initial amount 80; growth of 5% per input time unit.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
