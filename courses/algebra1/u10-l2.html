Algebra 1 / 10.2 · Read two-way tables

10.2 · Read two-way tables

Learning target

Compute joint and conditional relative frequencies.

Think about the idea

A two-way table records counts for two categorical variables. A joint frequency describes one cell; marginal totals sit at the edges. Conditional relative frequency divides by the total for the specified subgroup. State the denominator because changing it changes the question.

Worked example

Among 20 bus riders, 12 arrive early. Among 30 walkers, 24 arrive early. Compare early-arrival rates.

  1. Bus riders: 12/20 = 60%.
  2. Walkers: 24/30 = 80%.
  3. The overall early rate is 36/50 = 72%. These groups show an association, not proof that travel mode causes punctuality.

Spot and repair an error

A learner divides 12 by 50 to find the early-arrival rate among bus riders.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The phrase among bus riders restricts the denominator to 20. 12/50 answers a joint-frequency question instead.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Of 40 club members, 10 play chess. Find the proportion.

Check the reasoning

10/40=25%.

Practice 2

Among 15 morning students, 9 use a planner. Find the conditional rate.

Check the reasoning

9/15=60%; the denominator is the morning subgroup.

Practice 3

A table has 18 yes and 12 no responses in one row. Find that row total and yes rate.

Check the reasoning

Total 30; yes rate 18/30=60%.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
