Algebra 1 / 9.4 · Use a quadratic model

9.4 · Use a quadratic model

Learning target

Interpret a quadratic model and choose feasible solutions.

Think about the idea

A model’s roots, vertex, and intercept answer different questions. Time or length often excludes negative solutions. A parabola’s range depends on its domain; physical motion may end at the first ground hit. Units and assumptions belong in the conclusion.

Worked example

A simulated ball height is h(t) = −5t² + 20t meters from launch. Find maximum height and return time.

  1. Rewrite as h(t)=−5(t−2)²+20: vertex (2,20).
  2. The maximum is 20 meters at 2 seconds.
  3. Set h=0: −5t(t−4)=0, so launch is t=0 and return is t=4. Use 0≤t≤4.

Spot and repair an error

A learner says the maximum occurs at t=4 because that is the larger root.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Roots give ground-level times. The vertex gives the maximum, halfway between the two roots here.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

For h(t)=−t²+6t, when is maximum height reached?

Check the reasoning

At t=3; h(t)=−(t−3)²+9.

Practice 2

A rectangle has sides x and x+2 and area 24. Find positive dimensions.

Check the reasoning

x²+2x−24=0 gives x=4 or −6. Use x=4, so dimensions are 4 and 6.

Practice 3

For y=(x−1)²−9, find the real-number range.

Check the reasoning

y ≥ −9 because the square is nonnegative.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
