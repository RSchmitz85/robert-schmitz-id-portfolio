Algebra 1 / 9.3 · Complete the square and use the formula

9.3 · Complete the square and use the formula

Learning target

Choose a method to solve a quadratic and classify real roots.

Think about the idea

Completing the square rewrites x²+bx by adding and subtracting (b/2)². The quadratic formula x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac))/(2a) solves ax²+bx+c=0 with a≠0. A positive discriminant gives two distinct real roots, zero gives one repeated real root, and a negative value gives no real roots.

Worked example

Solve x² + 4x + 1 = 0 by completing the square.

  1. Move the constant: x² + 4x = −1.
  2. Add 4 to both sides: (x+2)² = 3.
  3. x = −2 ± √3. The formula gives the same result because the discriminant is 16−4=12.

Spot and repair an error

A learner writes the formula with denominator 2 but forgets a.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The entire numerator must be divided by 2a. Keep parentheses around numerator and denominator during substitution.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Complete the square: x² + 6x + 2.

Check the reasoning

(x+3)² − 7: add and subtract 9.

Practice 2

Solve x² − 2x − 1 = 0.

Check the reasoning

x = 1 ± √2, using the quadratic formula or (x−1)²=2.

Practice 3

How many distinct real roots does x² + 2x + 5 = 0 have?

Check the reasoning

None: discriminant 4−20=−16 is negative.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
