4.3 · Choose a meaningful domain

Learning target

Distinguish mathematical domains from contextual restrictions.

Think about the idea

The domain is the set of permitted inputs; the range is the set of resulting outputs. A formula can accept more inputs than its context allows. Counts are discrete whole numbers. Measurements may be continuous. Denominators cannot equal zero in a real-valued formula.

Worked example

A ticket seller receives R(n) = 8n dollars for n tickets, with capacity 100.

  1. Tickets are counted: n is an integer.
  2. The domain is {0, 1, 2, …, 100}.
  3. The range is {0, 8, 16, …, 800} dollars. It does not include every real number between 0 and 800.

Spot and repair an error

A learner includes 2.5 tickets because the formula gives 20 dollars.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The calculation is defined, but the input does not represent a whole ticket in this context.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Give a real-number domain for f(x) = 1/(x − 3).

Check the reasoning

All real x except 3, because x = 3 would make the denominator zero.

Practice 2

Water fills continuously for 6 minutes. Give a time domain starting at 0.

Check the reasoning

0 ≤ t ≤ 6, including fractional minutes.

Practice 3

Give the range for f(x) = x² on domain {−2, 0, 3}.

Check the reasoning

{0, 4, 9}, found by squaring each allowed input.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
