Algebra 1 / 10.4 · Choose and defend a model

10.4 · Choose and defend a model

Learning target

Compare linear, exponential, and quadratic patterns and communicate limits.

Think about the idea

For equally spaced inputs, constant first differences suggest a linear pattern, constant ratios suggest an exponential pattern, and constant second differences suggest a quadratic pattern. A short data set can fit many rules, so also consider the mechanism and residuals. Correlation describes association and cannot by itself establish causation.

Worked example

Outputs at x=0,1,2,3 are 1,4,9,16. Choose a simple model.

  1. First differences are 3,5,7; they are not constant.
  2. Second differences are 2,2, suggesting a quadratic pattern.
  3. y=(x+1)² matches all four points. More data and context are needed to support prediction beyond them.

Spot and repair an error

A learner says a high correlation proves one variable causes the other.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

A lurking variable, reverse direction, or a nonexperimental selection process could explain the association.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Classify outputs 3,6,12,24 at equally spaced inputs.

Check the reasoning

Exponential pattern: each output is multiplied by 2.

Practice 2

Classify outputs 2,7,12,17 at equally spaced inputs.

Check the reasoning

Linear pattern: constant first difference 5.

Practice 3

Ice cream sales and swimming both rise in warm months. Is causation established?

Check the reasoning

No. Temperature can affect both; association alone does not identify a causal relationship.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
