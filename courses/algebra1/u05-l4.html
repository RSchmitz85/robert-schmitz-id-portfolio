5.4 · Interpret and compare models

Learning target

Compare linear models with units and contextual bounds.

Think about the idea

A model is useful only within conditions that make sense. Interpret both slope and intercept with units. Compare outputs at the same input. The x-intercept solves y = 0, but a negative or fractional result may not be feasible for the context.

Worked example

Plan A costs A(h) = 20 + 5h dollars; Plan B costs B(h) = 8h. Compare at 4 and 10 hours.

  1. At h = 4: A = $40 and B = $32, so B is cheaper.
  2. At h = 10: A = $70 and B = $80, so A is cheaper.
  3. A starts higher but rises more slowly. The better choice depends on the number of hours.

Spot and repair an error

A learner selects Plan A for every customer because its hourly rate is lower.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

A fixed fee can outweigh a lower rate for short rentals. Compare complete costs.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

For V(t) = 60 − 3t liters, interpret both constants.

Check the reasoning

60 liters initially; volume decreases by 3 liters/minute if t is in minutes.

Practice 2

When does that tank reach 0 liters?

Check the reasoning

At t = 20 minutes. The draining model is physically limited to 0 ≤ t ≤ 20.

Practice 3

Compare C₁(x) = 10 + 2x and C₂(x) = 4x at x = 8.

Check the reasoning

C₁(8) = 26 and C₂(8) = 32, so C₁ is cheaper by 6 currency units.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
