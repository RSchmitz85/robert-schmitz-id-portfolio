2.3 · Identify special solution sets

Learning target

Distinguish one solution, no solution, and all real numbers.

Think about the idea

After simplifying, an equation may lose its variable terms. A false statement such as 4 = 9 means no input can work. A true statement such as 4 = 4 means all permitted inputs work. These are solution sets, not failures of a solving procedure.

Worked example

Compare 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6 with 2(x + 3) = 2x + 7.

  1. The first simplifies to 2x + 6 = 2x + 6, then 6 = 6.
  2. It is true for all real numbers.
  3. The second simplifies to 6 = 7, so it has no solution.

Spot and repair an error

A learner reports x = 0 whenever the x terms cancel.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Cancellation leaves a statement to evaluate. Test x = 1 to see why all-real and no-solution cases differ from x = 0.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Classify 4x + 1 = 4x − 2.

Check the reasoning

No solution, because subtracting 4x gives 1 = −2.

Practice 2

Classify 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6.

Check the reasoning

All real numbers, because both sides are equivalent.

Practice 3

Classify 4x + 1 = 2x + 1.

Check the reasoning

Exactly one solution, x = 0; subtracting 2x and 1 gives 2x = 0.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
