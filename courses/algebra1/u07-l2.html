Algebra 1 / 7.2 · Connect radicals and powers

7.2 · Connect radicals and powers

Learning target

Interpret square roots and rational exponents over real numbers.

Think about the idea

The principal square root √a is the nonnegative real number whose square is a, for a ≥ 0. The exponent 1/2 means that square root. Simplify a radical by extracting perfect-square factors. Solving x² = a requires both signs when a is positive, unlike evaluating √a.

Worked example

Simplify √72 and compare √36 with solutions of x² = 36.

  1. 72 = 36 × 2, so √72 = 6√2.
  2. √36 = 6 is the principal square root.
  3. The equation x² = 36 has solutions x = 6 and x = −6.

Spot and repair an error

A learner states √25 = ±5.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The radical symbol names the principal root 5. Both ±5 solve the different statement x² = 25.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Evaluate 81^(1/2).

Check the reasoning

9, the principal square root of 81.

Practice 2

Simplify √50.

Check the reasoning

5√2, because 50 = 25 × 2.

Practice 3

Solve x² = 49 over the real numbers.

Check the reasoning

x = −7 or x = 7; both square to 49.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
