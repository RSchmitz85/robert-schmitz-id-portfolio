8.1 · Add and subtract polynomials

Learning target

Combine polynomials while preserving signs and powers.

Think about the idea

A polynomial is a sum of terms with nonnegative whole-number exponents on its variables. Combine terms with the same variable powers. Subtracting a polynomial means adding the opposite of every term in it. Writing in descending powers helps organize the result.

Worked example

Simplify (3x² + 2x − 1) − (x² − 4x + 5).

  1. Distribute the subtraction: 3x² + 2x − 1 − x² + 4x − 5.
  2. Combine like powers: (3−1)x² + (2+4)x − 6.
  3. The result is 2x² + 6x − 6.

Spot and repair an error

A learner changes the sign of x² but leaves −4x and +5 unchanged.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

Subtraction applies to the whole polynomial; reverse every term’s sign.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Add (x² + 3x) + (2x² − x + 4).

Check the reasoning

3x² + 2x + 4.

Practice 2

Subtract (5y − 2) − (3y + 7).

Check the reasoning

2y − 9: 5y − 2 − 3y − 7.

Practice 3

Can 3x² + 2x be combined to 5x³?

Check the reasoning

No. Unlike powers are unlike terms; the original sum must remain.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
