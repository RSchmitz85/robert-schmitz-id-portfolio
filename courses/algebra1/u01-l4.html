Algebra 1 / 1.4 · Write expressions from situations

1.4 · Write expressions from situations

Learning target

Represent a situation and interpret the parts of an expression.

Think about the idea

Define a variable before writing an expression. A fixed amount is added once; a per-item amount multiplies the number of items. Read the expression back into the situation to check the model. A context may restrict inputs to nonnegative whole numbers.

Worked example

A club pays a $25 setup fee and $6 per shirt. Write and interpret the total cost for n shirts.

  1. Let n be the number of shirts, a nonnegative whole number.
  2. The total cost in dollars is 25 + 6n.
  3. For 8 shirts the cost is 25 + 48 = $73; 25 is fixed and 6 is dollars per shirt.

Spot and repair an error

A learner models the cost as 25n + 6.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

That charges the setup fee for every shirt. The quantity paid for every shirt is $6.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Write the cost of a $12 ticket plus $3 per game for g games.

Check the reasoning

12 + 3g dollars, for nonnegative whole-number g.

Practice 2

Interpret 40 − 2t as liters remaining after t minutes.

Check the reasoning

The tank begins with 40 liters and loses 2 liters per minute; the physical model applies for 0 ≤ t ≤ 20.

Practice 3

A rectangle has width w and length w + 3. Write its perimeter.

Check the reasoning

2w + 2(w + 3) = 4w + 6; w must be positive.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
