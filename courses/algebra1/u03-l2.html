Algebra 1 / 3.2 · Solve linear inequalities

3.2 · Solve linear inequalities

Learning target

Solve an inequality and explain a direction reversal.

Think about the idea

Add or subtract the same quantity on both sides as with equations. Multiplication or division by a negative number reverses order. For example, 2 < 5 becomes −2 > −5 after multiplication by −1. Verify a proposed solution set using a test value.

Worked example

Solve −3x + 4 ≤ 13.

  1. Subtract 4: −3x ≤ 9.
  2. Divide by −3 and reverse the inequality: x ≥ −3.
  3. Check the boundary: 13 ≤ 13; check x = 0: 4 ≤ 13. Both work.

Spot and repair an error

A learner writes x ≤ −3 after dividing by −3.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

A negative multiplier reverses order. x = 0 satisfies the original inequality but would be excluded by that answer.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Solve 2x + 1 < 9.

Check the reasoning

x < 4: subtract 1 and divide by positive 2.

Practice 2

Solve −4x > 12.

Check the reasoning

x < −3: divide by −4 and reverse direction.

Practice 3

Solve 5 − x ≥ 8.

Check the reasoning

x ≤ −3: subtract 5, then divide by −1 and reverse direction.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
