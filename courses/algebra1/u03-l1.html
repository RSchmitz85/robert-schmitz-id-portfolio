Algebra 1 / 3.1 · Represent solution sets

3.1 · Represent solution sets

Learning target

Interpret an inequality and represent its boundary.

Think about the idea

An inequality describes a range, not just a single answer. The symbols < and > exclude the boundary; ≤ and ≥ include it. On a number line use an open circle for an excluded boundary and a closed circle for an included one. Test values on either side.

Worked example

Interpret x ≥ −2.

  1. The boundary is −2 and is included.
  2. Use a closed circle at −2 and shade toward larger numbers.
  3. −2, 0, and 5 satisfy the inequality; −3 does not.

Spot and repair an error

A learner uses a closed circle for x > 4.

Before revealing the explanation, identify the first incorrect step and propose a repair.

Compare your repair

The value 4 makes 4 > 4 false, so the boundary must be open.

Your turn

Work each question independently. Include a method or explanation, then compare your reasoning.

Practice 1

Does −1 satisfy x < −3?

Check the reasoning

No. −1 is greater than −3.

Practice 2

Describe x ≤ 6 on a number line.

Check the reasoning

Closed circle at 6, shaded to the left.

Practice 3

Write a constraint for a height h of at least 48 inches.

Check the reasoning

h ≥ 48 inches; the boundary is included.

Reflect before moving on

Explain one decision you made and how you checked it. If your answer changed after checking, record the original error and your corrected reasoning.
