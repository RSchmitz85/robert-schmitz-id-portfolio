# Workplace feedback: learner pilot protocol

Status: prepared for a pilot; no participants have been tested and no results are reported.

## Question

After practicing the scenario, can a new manager write a useful response to a different missed handoff without answer options?

## Participants and session

Recruit 3–5 adults who are new to supervising others or preparing for that responsibility. Participation is voluntary. Use anonymous participant codes; do not collect employer, employee, or client names. Allow about 15 minutes per person. This small pilot identifies usability and transfer problems; it cannot establish workplace impact or general effectiveness.

## Before practice

Give this case: A teammate sends a weekly status summary after the agreed deadline. A colleague cannot finish the project update. You do not know why the summary was late.

Ask the participant to write their opening statement and one question. Then disclose: the teammate was waiting for a dependency from another team. Ask for a proposed next agreement. Record the response before giving any coaching.

## Practice

Open the portfolio workplace scenario. Ask the participant to work through it and say what they expect each choice to do. Do not suggest answers. Record unclear wording, unexpected feedback, difficulty finding the next action, and help required. Ask them to restart and explore another response if time permits.

## Transfer task

Give a new case without answer options: A client summary is late because two reviewers supplied conflicting edits. The receiving team needs an agreed version to prepare for a meeting.

Ask for an opening statement, a follow-up question, and a proposed agreement. Allow access to the conversation aid: this tests the scenario plus the aid, not unaided recall. Do not coach the response.

## Review rubric

Mark each element present, partly present, or absent. Keep the actual response alongside the rating.

| Element | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Specific event | Describes the agreed handoff and observed delay without a personal judgment. |
| Impact | Explains the consequence for the receiving team. |
| Inquiry | Asks an open question and checks the dependency instead of assuming its cause. |
| Workable agreement | Names an owner, time, early-warning action, and check-in; checks that the receiving team can use the proposed handoff. |

## Revision and repeat

Choose one observed problem, preserve the original wording or screen, and document the change and its reason. Test the revised experience with a new participant where possible. Record whether that specific difficulty recurs. If the same participant repeats the task, disclose the practice effect.

Report participant count, recruitment, tasks, help given, observations, changes, and unresolved questions. Do not turn a small usability pilot into a completion-rate, learning-gain, or business-impact claim.
